What this is. Tremor Lab runs the estimators of catalogue seismology: the completeness magnitude by maximum curvature (Wiemer & Wyss 2000), the b‑value by the Aki (1965) maximum‑likelihood estimator with the Shi & Bolt (1982) standard error, the modified Omori–Utsu decay by maximum likelihood (Ogata 1983; Utsu, Ogata & Matsuura 1995) optimised with a Nelder–Mead simplex, radiated energy log10E = 1.5M + 4.8, the Båth energy screen, and magnitude homogenisation to Mw by the Scordilis (2006) relations.

Which numbers to publish. This page runs the JavaScript implementation. The companion Python package is the authority for published values; the two agree to three decimals on the reference catalogue, and the self‑test above shows this page reproducing all eight of those values live.

Five deliberate differences from the spreadsheet version. The bootstrap is seeded, so the same seed gives the same error bars instead of a new answer each run. Times are handled in UTC, so elapsed days do not depend on the viewer’s time zone or on daylight saving. The decay search restarts from several starting points, because one start can settle short of the best fit. A converged decay fit is marked when c collapses onto the boundary of the model or p falls outside the range reported for real sequences. And the minimum event counts follow the Python package — more than 50 events for a completeness magnitude, more than 100 above the threshold for a b‑value or a decay fit — rather than the spreadsheet’s lower ones.
