## Comparison with published results for the 2023 Kahramanmaraş sequence

### The structural problem, stated first

Every published value I was able to verify for this sequence comes from a catalogue that is not the one the thesis analyses. The comparison set is: AFAD's national catalogue (Tan 2025; Hainzl, Kumazawa & Ogata 2024; Kwiatek et al. 2023; Nanjo et al. 2025), the Lomax (2023) relocated catalogue (Rodríguez-Pérez & Zúñiga 2025; Nanjo et al. 2025 as a cross-check), a GFZ machine-learning catalogue (Becker et al. 2024), and one paper whose catalogue provenance is never stated at all (Ali & Abdelrahman 2024). I did not find a single published aftershock-window completeness magnitude or b-value computed on a KOERI catalogue for this sequence. The one KOERI-derived study I verified (Şenkaya et al. 2025, doi:10.1007/s11600-025-01622-5) splices KOERI ML events onto Tan's (2021) homogenised MW catalogue and reports Mc = 2.1 for a 118.5-year regional window in the Malatya/Kale area — a different agency mix, a different region, a different magnitude scale and a window that is not an aftershock window. That is not comparable to anything in the thesis and should not be presented as if it were.

The consequence is that none of the numbers below can be set beside the Tremor Lab numbers as a like-for-like check. They can support or fail to support the *shape* of what we measure. They cannot confirm or contradict a value.

### Does published work support a time-varying completeness magnitude?

Yes, clearly and from three independent directions.

Tan (2025, *Acta Geophysica* 73:1023–1040, doi:10.1007/s11600-024-01419-y) is the closest published analogue in form. Working on the AFAD national catalogue — 50,085 events, 6 February to 31 October 2023, Mc by maximum likelihood after Wiemer (2001) — he reports that completeness "decreases gradually from ~ 3.0 on the first day to ~ 2.0 two weeks later," then "converges to the constant value of ~ 1.5 after mid-March and is 1.6 for the nine-month catalog data." That is a first-day Mc roughly 1.4 magnitude units above the whole-window value, measured on one catalogue by one method — the same asymmetry Tremor Lab reports (3.8 in the first day against 3.4 for the whole window, a difference of 0.4). The direction and the recovery over weeks match. The magnitudes of the offset do not, and I would not claim they should.

Hainzl, Kumazawa & Ogata (2024, *GJI* 236:1609–1620, doi:10.1093/gji/ggae006) attack the same problem differently. On the AFAD catalogue in the box 33.5–41.0° E, 34.0–40.0° N, 1 January to 8 March 2023, they estimate detection completeness two independent ways (the Ogata–Katsura 1993 model and the ETASI detection-probability function) and report that "with the occurrence of the largest aftershock, the completeness magnitude jumps up and then decays faster than expected from the empirical relationship," and that "the 90 per cent probability curve is always about one magnitude larger than the 50 per cent curve, indicating that many earthquakes are still missed between the two curves." They also state plainly that although their catalogue "is complete for m ≥ 2 events according to the OK93 model before the mainshock, it is incomplete for the first few days after the mainshock." Note that their conclusion is *stronger* than ours: they argue that any time-dependent threshold, including a time-varying Mc, is insufficient, and that a detection-probability function is required. Our band-by-band Mc is a coarser instrument than what they recommend. That should be conceded in the text rather than glossed.

Third, the GFZ data description for the machine-learning aftershock catalogues (Becker, Martínez-Garzón, McBrearty & Beroza 2024, doi:10.5880/GFZ.4.2.2024.001) — 38 permanent AFAD and KOERI stations, 6 February 01:18 UTC to 11 February 2023 — states that the magnitude range is "between M-0.1 and M6.9 with time-variable magnitude of completeness." That is qualitative only; no numeric Mc(t) appears anywhere in that four-page document, and I did not fetch the companion BSSA paper, so no number should be cited from it.

So: the claim that completeness is not constant in time in this sequence is well supported in the published literature, and the direction of change (worst immediately after the mainshock, recovering over days to weeks) is supported. The specific values we report are not corroborated by anything, because nothing published uses our catalogue.

### Does any published Mc for this sequence sit above M 3.5 in the first day?

Not as a measured value, in anything I verified. This is the honest answer and it is a weakness in the argument as currently framed.

Tan's first-day AFAD figure is ~3.0 — below the M 3.5 threshold the thesis uses, not above it. Hainzl et al.'s ETASI cut-off for the whole fit is Mc = 1.95. Kwiatek et al. (2023, *Nat. Commun.* 14:7534, doi:10.1038/s41467-023-42419-8) give Mc = 1.5 for the AFAD catalogue within 130 km of the epicentre from 2014 to the mainshock — pre-mainshock only, and must not be quoted as an aftershock value.

Two published statements come close without answering the question:

The nearest thing to support is a *prediction*, not a measurement. Hainzl et al. write that "the empirical relation of Helmstetter et al. (2006) predicts that m = 4 aftershocks are not fully recorded within the first 2 hours after a M = 7.7 earthquake, such as the 2023 Türkiye event, while m = 3 events are only partially recorded, if at all, within the first 2 d." That does bracket our first-band Mc 3.9 (29 minutes to 2.4 hours) and our 2.4-hour-to-1-day Mc 3.6. But it is an extrapolation of a relation the same authors flag as "only been calibrated to mainshocks in California," applied to AFAD data, and the authors then argue against using it. It supports the plausibility of a first-day Mc near 4 for *some* catalogue of this sequence. It is not a measurement, and it is not our catalogue.

The only published Mc for this sequence above 3.5 is Ali & Abdelrahman (2024, *Fractal Fract.* 8(5):252, doi:10.3390/fractalfract8050252): Mc = 4.4 by maximum curvature in ZMAP 6.0, for a 471-event catalogue spanning M 3.3–7.8 over 6 February 2023 to 10 January 2024 in 35.0–39.0° N / 34.0–41.0° E. That is a whole-window value, not a first-day value, and the paper never names the agency that supplied the catalogue (IRIS is described at length in the paragraph immediately before, but never asserted as the source, and the magnitude scale is never given). It illustrates that a coarse global catalogue can have an Mc a full unit above the threshold we use, but it does not bear on the first day.

I would therefore write the thesis claim as: *no published study reports a measured first-day completeness magnitude above 3.5 for this sequence; the published first-day value on the AFAD catalogue is ~3.0 (Tan 2025), and the only published statement putting incompleteness above M 3.5 in the first hours is an extrapolated empirical prediction (Helmstetter et al. 2006, as applied in Hainzl et al. 2024).* That is weaker than "published work confirms our first-day Mc," and it is what the record supports.

### On the gap between our absolute Mc values and the published ones

Our whole-window Mc of 3.4 sits roughly 1.8 units above Tan's 1.6 for the same sequence. The obvious explanation is that our catalogue is truncated at M 3.0, so nothing below 3.0 can be seen and no completeness estimate below that floor is possible. This explanation is our own inference, not a claim made by Tan, who never mentions KOERI and never compares his numbers to any other catalogue. It should be written in our own voice. A related methodological caution, also ours: a maximum-curvature estimate computed on a catalogue whose lowest magnitude is 3.0 is working over a very narrow range of magnitudes, and the +0.2 correction was not calibrated for that situation. That deserves a sentence acknowledging it rather than being left for a reader to notice.

For context on how much of a completeness level is a network-and-processing property rather than a property of the earthquakes: Kwiatek et al. report Mc = 0.48 for their deep-learning (PhaseNet/GaMMA) catalogue against Mc = 1.45 for AFAD, over the same 50 km radius and the same 1 January to 6 February 2023 window — 252 events above completeness against 22. Again, the interpretive use of that pair is ours, not theirs.

### b-values: what can and cannot be compared

Our b = 0.844 ± 0.019 at the published M 3.5 threshold has no directly comparable published counterpart. The candidates and why each fails:

- Tan reports the Gutenberg–Richter slope as "about 0.8" for the Pazarcık and Elbistan distributions, one significant figure, no uncertainty, and does not restate the Mc used for that particular fit. The catalogue's Mc is 1.6.
- Rodríguez-Pérez & Zúñiga (2025, *Acta Geophysica* 73:1241–1260, doi:10.1007/s11600-024-01428-x) give coseismic-stage b of 0.84 on the EAFZ and 0.9 on the Çardak fault. They state Mc was estimated by maximum curvature but never print a number anywhere in the paper. Without a stated completeness these values cannot be placed on a comparable axis at all.
- Hainzl et al.'s ETASI value is b = 0.87 at Mc = 1.95 on AFAD.
- Ali & Abdelrahman's b = 1.21 is at Mc = 4.4, nearly a full magnitude unit above our threshold, and the paper is internally inconsistent about its own uncertainty (±0.03 in the results text, ±0.1 in the abstract and conclusions).

The numerical proximity of our 0.844 to Tan's ~0.8 and to Rodríguez-Pérez & Zúñiga's 0.84 is a coincidence of different catalogues at different (or unstated) completeness levels and should not be presented as agreement.

One published number is worth citing, with its own caveat, next to our b-value-stability Mc of 4.1. Hainzl et al. report that for the background catalogue in their region (AFAD, 2000–2022, *not* the aftershock sequence), "the b estimate becomes stable within its uncertainties for Mc > 3.5 and scatters around 1.07." That is the same criterion in the same spirit, and it lands close to the threshold the thesis uses. But it is background seismicity over 23 years, not the 2023 aftershocks, so it is a family resemblance and not a check; and the same authors say elsewhere that "a stable estimation of b is difficult for this data set." Cite the number and the caution together, or a referee holding the paper will supply the caution for you.

### The band-by-band b trend runs the wrong way against the best published estimate

This is the finding the thesis most needs to address, and it should be raised by us rather than by an examiner.

We measure b rising with time: 0.737 ± 0.080 in the first hours, 0.723 ± 0.034 to one day, then 0.893, 0.932 and 1.018 across the later bands. Hainzl et al., using the Ogata–Katsura (1993) estimator — which fits b together with a time-varying detection function instead of assuming a fixed threshold — find the opposite sense on the AFAD catalogue: "b = 0.76 ± 0.07 for the earthquakes that occurred before the mainshock and b values of about 1.2 for the early aftershocks, decaying to about 0.85 for the later aftershocks," settling by about ten days to a value consistent with the constant b = 0.87 of their ETASI fit. They describe it as a roughly 50 per cent coseismic increase in b.

Our reading is that this is a completeness artefact in our own numbers: an Aki estimate at a fixed M 3.5 threshold, in bands whose measured Mc is 3.9 and 3.6, is biased and the bias is largest exactly where our b is lowest. That reading is well founded on the mechanism Hainzl et al. document, but it is our inference. They do not analyse our data, they work at Mc = 1.95 on a different catalogue, and they make no claim about the Tremor Lab result. The text should say so explicitly, and should treat this as a result the thesis has to reconcile rather than as a result it is supported by.

### Omori parameters: one near-comparison, and the rest not comparable

Our p = 1.161, c = 0.497 d, k = 358.9 at M 3.5 over 180 days.

The nearest published fit in form is Nanjo et al. (2025, *Geosciences* 15(4):113, doi:10.3390/geosciences15040113), who fit λ = k(c+t)^-p to the AFAD catalogue at three thresholds over 618 days from the M7.7: at M ≥ 3, p = 1.11 ± 0.01, c = 1.303 ± 0.12 d, k = 1042.94 ± 67.18; at M ≥ 4, p = 1.20 ± 0.03, c = 0.331 ± 0.064 d, k = 145.31 ± 15.03; at M ≥ 2, c = 12.604 ± 0.6 d. Two things follow. First, their own three thresholds show c moving by nearly two orders of magnitude with the cut-off — which is exactly the sensitivity the thesis is arguing about, though Nanjo et al. never discuss it and never attribute it to early incompleteness, so that reading must be ours. Second, our c = 0.497 d at M 3.5 falls between their M ≥ 3 and M ≥ 4 values, which is suggestive but is not a comparison: different agency, a 618-day window against our 180, and their values were read off Figure 13 rather than from text.

The other published Omori fits cannot be compared to ours at all. Tan's p = 1.0, c = 5 d, k = 2600 for the EAFZ and p = 0.7, c = 5 d, k = 930 for Çardak are fitted to *daily count histograms* on an AFAD catalogue truncated at M 2.0, with no uncertainties reported. Rodríguez-Pérez & Zúñiga's p = 1.25 ± 0.08 (EAFZ) and 1.14 ± 0.09 (Çardak) come from the Lomax catalogue at an unstated completeness. Ali & Abdelrahman's p = 1.1 ± 0.04, c = 0.204 ± 0.058 d, k = 76.75 ± 8.84 are at Mc 4.4 on an unattributed catalogue; that paper also misinterprets its own k as a number of days, so its Omori discussion should not be leaned on even where its fitted numbers are quoted correctly. In every case k is not comparable across different thresholds, since it scales with the number of events above the cut.

### Precedent for dropping the first day

There is one, and it is worth citing carefully. Nanjo et al. fit ETAS to M ≥ 3.0 AFAD events with "the ETAS fitting in the target interval from 1.5 days after the M7.7 quake to 16 October 2024, where the precursor interval is from the moment of the M7.7 quake to 1.5 days after the M7.7 quake." So a peer-reviewed ETAS study of this exact sequence does exclude the first 1.5 days from the fitted interval, which is precedent for our decision to test dropping day 1 (which moves our b from 0.844 to 0.947).

Two qualifications. The paper gives no reason for the 1.5-day choice — a search of the full text turns up no discussion of completeness, detection capability or missing events in connection with it — so it corroborates the *practice* and not the *rationale*. And in ETAS a precursory interval is not discarded data: events in it still act as triggering history in the likelihood, they are simply outside the fitted target window. Our procedure, which removes the events, is not the same operation and should not be described as matching theirs.

### One decay curve or two

The literature is genuinely split, and a thesis fitting one Omori curve to the whole doublet should say so. Nanjo et al. conclude that "the standard (single) ETAS model (red curve) fits the data after the M7.7 quake well," verified at M ≥ 4 and against the Lomax catalogue. Tan reaches the opposite conclusion for the second rupture: "the best MOL model (p = 0.7, c = 5, k = 930) for the Elbistan aftershocks has discrepancies for the first and second half of the observation period," and "although a simple decay model explains the aftershock activity on the EAFZ, it cannot be enough for the Elbistan mainshock." Rodríguez-Pérez & Zúñiga treat the two faults separately by design but do not argue that a single curve fails, so they are weaker support for the sceptical position than Tan is; do not present the two papers as making the same argument.

### On extrapolating a 180-day fit

Nanjo et al. estimate the sequence duration at "2.7–5.5 years, which is longer than previously proposed (1–2.5 years)" — the earlier figure being Toda and Stein's, and the difference attributed to fitting window length, 618 days against about 100. That is a direct caution against reading long-term behaviour out of a short fit, and a 180-day window is closer to the shorter of those two. (I have not read Toda and Stein directly; the 1–2.5 year figure is Nanjo et al.'s report of it, and should be cited that way or checked at source.)

### What remains unchecked, and what to look up by hand

Read these yourself before submission:

1. **Hainzl et al., Figure 2(b).** This is the single most valuable missing number. Their 50 per cent and 90 per cent detection curves are plotted against time; reading the 90 per cent curve at day 1 would give a measured (not extrapolated) published completeness level for the first day on the AFAD catalogue, which is exactly what the thesis argument lacks. I have not read a numeric value off that figure and none should be quoted until you have.
2. **Tan, Figure 12 (Appendix)**, the Mc-versus-time plot. The ~3.0 and ~2.0 values are in the text and verified; the shape of the curve between them is only in the figure.
3. **The Ali & Abdelrahman uncertainty.** The paper prints b = 1.21 ± 0.1 in the abstract and conclusions and ± 0.03 in the results. Either cite ± 0.1 or state the discrepancy. Their catalogue provenance is unstated; describe it as unattributed (IRIS implied by adjacency), not as an IRIS catalogue.
4. **Nanjo et al., Figure 13.** The p, c, k values were read off an image, not text. Verify them in the published figure at full resolution before quoting, and note that the paper labels the doublet M7.7/M7.5, not M7.8/M7.6 — quote their labels or a reader will not find the numbers.
5. **The KOERI-to-AFAD magnitude question.** Nothing verified establishes how KOERI ML for this sequence relates to AFAD ML or to MW. Şenkaya et al. use Tan's (2021) MW = 1.017 ML − 0.012 for a homogenised catalogue; its applicability to our extract is untested. Until that is settled, every cross-agency comparison in this section carries an unquantified magnitude-scale offset, and the section should say so once, plainly.
6. **Becker et al.'s companion BSSA paper** (doi:10.1785/0120240017) was not fetched. Any numeric Mc(t) for the first five days would be there. Cite nothing from it until read.
7. **Helmstetter et al. (2006)** was not read at source; the relation Mc(m,t) = m − 4.5 − 0.75 log(t) and its California calibration are known here only through Hainzl et al.
8. **Year for Tan.** Online-first 2024, issue 2025. Pick one and be consistent.
9. **Not found, and worth one more manual search:** any published aftershock-window Mc or b computed on a KOERI catalogue for this sequence. I did not find one. A web search surfaced a *J. Earth Syst. Sci.* paper (Akar 2024, doi:10.1007/s12040-024-02398-w) on spatio-temporal b-value changes for this earthquake; I fetched it and it names neither its catalogue source nor a numeric Mc in the accessible text, so nothing from it is usable. Search summaries also floated a pre-mainshock Mc of 1.8 by MAXC and a pre-mainshock b of 0.71 ± 0.04 from a paper I did not fetch; those numbers are unverified and must not enter the thesis on that basis.

### Attributions that must stay in our own voice

Four claims in this section are interpretations, not findings of the papers cited, and the text should make that explicit: that Tan's time-varying Mc is the analogue of our band-by-band result; that the ~1.8-unit offset between our Mc and Tan's is caused by our M 3.0 truncation; that our reversed band-by-band b trend against Hainzl et al. is a completeness artefact; and that Nanjo et al.'s 1.5-day exclusion is motivated by early incompleteness. Each is defensible reasoning. None is something the cited author says.
